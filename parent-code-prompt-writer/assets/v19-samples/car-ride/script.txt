Some of the best conversations with teenagers don't happen face to face.
They happen in the car.
Nobody has to make eye contact.
Nobody can storm off.
And the road gives everyone somewhere to look.
Last week, Lily's grandmother picked her up from football practice.
Lily got in, slammed the door, and said nothing.
Grandma didn't ask what was wrong.
She just turned the radio down.
She'd had this conversation before — with Lily's mum, years ago.
Ten minutes later: "Coach dropped me from the team."
That's the moment most of us jump in.
"Did you talk to him? Maybe if you trained harder…"
Grandma said: "That's really hard."
Then she kept driving.
And Lily kept talking.
Not because Grandma had the right answer.
Because the car felt safe.
Sometimes the bravest thing a parent can do is keep their eyes on the road.
