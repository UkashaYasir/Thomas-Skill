# Acting and interaction — faces, bodies and people together (v19)

Read with `v19-principles.md` §3. Where older files (`emotion-and-performance.md`, `characters.md`) say something
different about performance or interaction, this file wins.

**This is an open library.** Every list below is seed examples plus a method for making new ones. It is never a menu
to pick from. When a line needs an expression, an interaction or a behaviour that is not here, build it with the
method in that section. After every video, add the new entries that worked.

---

## 0. What Thomas asked for (verbatim)

- Faces: "This is one of the biggest areas we can still improve. Please use more: eyes, eyebrows, mouth movement, head
  position, hand gestures, posture, eye direction. The characters should not just "stand in the scene." They should
  clearly react."
- "What I still feel is missing is stronger emotion. I want more from the facial expressions, the eyes, the mouth, the
  hands, the body language, and the closeness to the camera."
- Instead of symbols: "Instead of simple symbols, show emotions through: eye contact, hesitation, body language,
  distance between people, hands, reactions, silence, facial expressions, real interactions."
- Love and connection: "If we want to show love, trust, attention, or emotional safety, I would prefer situations like:
  a parent quietly sitting next to a teenager; a teen looking away but still listening; a hand reaching out; a parent
  stopping before reacting; a child hesitating before telling the truth; someone noticing the other person is
  uncomfortable; a parent kneeling down to the child's level; two people sitting together without needing a heart
  symbol. This feels much more real and emotionally powerful."
- Final priorities include: "stronger facial expressions; more real interaction between characters; … stronger gestures
  and body language".

**What the last video did** (R2 §2–3, "Seven Things"; measurements, not targets): eyebrows, mouth, pupils and hands
were named in almost every performance, but head position and posture rarely were. Most eye directions were a plain
"pupils on MOM / pupils on SON". Some characters just stood with their arms at their sides. Many multi-person frames had
no gaze, no touch and no reach at all. Distance was written as a stock phrase ("an arm's length away") instead of being
used as a feeling. The "seriously?" look was rare and the awkward pause never appeared. This file fixes those gaps.

## 1. The face and body we act with

The construction comes from the MOM and SON reference images and never changes (see `characters.md` and
`cast-design-v19.md`):
- a round white head with a bold black outline and small half-round ears;
- big white round eyes, each with a large solid black pupil;
- short, thick black eyebrows — straight dashes with rounded ends;
- a mouth drawn as a clear shape (never a tiny neutral dash), with **no teeth, ever**;
- one thin black line for the body; every arm and leg is one thin line with no width and no fill;
- white mitten hands with one thumb bump and no cuff; small white oval feet;
- no clothing — only the small accessories where needed a ROLE names. No nose (MOM's profile point only), no eyelashes, no cheeks
  or blush marks.

Because the figures are this simple, every feeling is built from a few dials. Turn them hard.

### 1.1 The seven features as mechanics

Thomas's seven features, in his order. Each one is a set of dials. Describe the dial positions, not the feeling.

**1. Eyes** (the shape of the eye itself)
- Wide open: the full white circle, white showing all round the pupil (alarm, shock, fear, realisation).
- Narrowed: a straight upper-lid line cutting across the top of the circle (suspicion, anger, the "seriously?" look —
  often one eye only).
- Heavy: the lid line low, the eye half closed (tired, bored, unimpressed, sleepy).
- Shut: each eye drawn as a short curved lid line (relief, laughing, bracing, a slow breath).
- Soft: the circle relaxed, the lid line just touching the top of the pupil (warmth, tenderness).
- A blink, a squeeze or a widening is an image edit on the next still, not a description of motion.
- Sadness comes from brows, lids, head and posture. No tears.

**2. Eyebrows** (always two short straight dashes; only height, tilt and gap move)
- Height: lifted high above the eye / level / pressed low onto the top of the eye.
- Tilt: inner ends up (worry, sadness, pleading, guilt) / inner ends down (anger, determination, concentration).
- Gap: pinched together (strain, worry, anger) / wide apart (surprise, openness).
- Asymmetry: one dash lifted, the other level or low (doubt, the "seriously?" look, sceptical amusement).

**3. Mouth movement** (always a shape; "movement" means the mouth is caught mid-change)
- Closed shapes: a wide flat pressed line (clearly wider than the neutral dash); a small tight down-turned arc; a deep
  frown curve; a wavering wavy line; a lopsided line pulled to one corner; a soft small smile curve; a big closed
  smile.
- Open shapes (plain black inside, no teeth): a small round "o"; an open oval; a wide open calling or shouting oval; a
  big open laugh; a small open shape caught mid-word.
- Caught moving: half-open just before the first word; closing after the words stop; opening and then stopping
  ("…never mind").

**4. Head position**
- Tilt sideways (listening, curiosity, doubt, softness).
- Chin tucked (shame, hurt, sulking; with the eyes looking up it becomes defiance).
- Chin lifted (pride, defiance, looking down on someone).
- Head pulled back (surprise, recoil, disbelief).
- Head pushed forward (insistence, interrogation, aggression).
- Head bowed (defeat, guilt).
- Head turned away (avoidance) — and the head turned away while the eyes come back (listening without admitting it).
- Head sunk down between raised shoulders (fear, embarrassment, bracing).
- Cheek propped on one mitten, elbow on the table (boredom, exhaustion) — never the hand-on-chin "thinking" pose;
  thinking is a head tilt.

**5. Hand gestures** (one dominant gesture per character, held clear of the body so the silhouette reads)
- Open palm up (offering, pleading, "what did I do?").
- Palm out, flat (stop, boundary, "calm down") — a calm raised palm replaces any stop sign.
- Palm down, pressing the air (slow down, wait).
- Both palms raised beside the head (surrender, "I didn't say anything").
- The whole mitten aimed at someone (pointing — mittens have no fingers, so the whole hand points).
- Fist: a closed mitten (anger, determination, holding it in).
- Arms crossed tight (closed, defensive, unconvinced). Arms akimbo — hands planted at the sides, elbows out (impatience,
  authority). Write "arms akimbo", never "hands on hips".
- Mitten rubbing the back of the neck (embarrassment). Mitten over the mouth (shock, words held back). Both mittens
  over the face (shame). Mitten pressed to the forehead (stress).
- Hiding something behind the back; turning an object over and over (nerves); gripping an object too hard (tension).
- Hand hovering just short of touching; a reach; a hand dropping halfway (giving up); a hand raised to speak, then
  lowered.
- Hands wrapped round the knees (making oneself small); hands round a mug (comfort, holding on).
- A prop that will be revealed with a mask sits apart from the hands; the hand points at it, reaches toward it or rests
  beside it.

**6. Posture** (what the body line does)
- Upright and straight (alert, proud, formal). Leaning forward (interest, pressure). Leaning back (withdrawal,
  dismissal, surprise).
- Slumped, the body line curved (defeat, tiredness). Curled small — knees up, arms wrapped (shame, overwhelm, hurt).
- Expansive — arms out, wide stance (confidence, anger, triumph).
- Frozen mid-step, one foot lifted (shock, being caught).
- Weight on one leg, body line tilted (casual, pretending not to care).
- Shoulders raised toward the ears (tension, fear, embarrassment). Shoulders dropped (relief, letting go). The
  shoulders are where the arms curve out from the top of the body line.
- Turned away, back toward the other person (rejection). Half-turned (torn, ambivalent).
- Sitting on the very edge of the seat (ready to leave, anxious). Stepping back. Stepping in. Kneeling or crouching to
  someone's level.

**7. Eye direction** (where the pupils sit inside the white circles)
- Pressed to a corner toward a target: the other person, the object, the door, the phone.
- Down (shame, avoidance, thinking). Up and pressed to the top (the eye roll). Sideways with the head still (side-eye).
- On the other person and returned (eye contact). On the other person and not returned. Both on the same object
  (shared attention).
- Away, then back — or on the person, then away — as an edit between two stills.
- The direction always matches the geography: if MOM is out of frame left, the pupils go left (eyeline match). In
  close shots this is the plan's `look` field ("toward MOM, out of frame left"). Never a look into the camera.

### 1.2 Face size decides what can be read

In WIDE and MEDWIDE frames the face is small. There the body carries the emotion — posture, hands, distance — and the
face keeps one bold shape (brows and mouth). Fine eye mechanics belong in CLOSE, XCLOSE, FACE_HANDS and REACTION
frames. If a beat depends on the eyes, move the camera closer (`camera-and-closeups-v19.md`). Never write a detailed
eye beat into a face that will be a few pixels wide.

### 1.3 The ceiling — stronger than life, never distorted

Thomas wants expressions stronger than real life. **Scale up the features, not the distortion.**
- Allowed: strongly tilted brows; brows pressed low or lifted high; pupils pushed hard into a corner; a large open
  oval mouth; a deep frown; a tight wide pressed line; a big clean smile; one straight lid line narrowing an eye; a
  head pulled right back; a body curled very small.
- Never: teeth (bared, gritted or smiling); a snarl; bulging or popping eyes; a stretched or warped face; tears;
  a lopsided smug grin with drooping lids; horror or rage caricature.
- No cartoon effects of any kind: sweat drops, steam, stars or swirls round the head, anger marks, motion lines, blush
  marks. They read as a kids' channel. The face and body do the work.

## 2. One clear emotion per face

Drawn faces are misread more often than we think. In a test of comic faces, readers reliably named only a few
expressions; blended faces were unpredictable (S10). So:
- **One emotion per face per frame.** Name it precisely, with its cause and target: not "sad" but "stung that MOM
  didn't look up".
- **Mixed feelings are split.** Either across two stills (guilty, then hopeful), or — when the beat is a mask — across
  two named channels of one body: the face shows the mask clearly and one other channel gives it away. Write which
  channel. Example, "pretending not to care": the face is flat and bored; only the pupils slide toward MOM.
- **Two people, two emotions.** The action and the reaction usually carry different feelings. That difference is the
  scene.
- **Never a blank face.** Quiet beats still have one emotion: wary, tired, guarded, softening, waiting.

## 3. Verbs, not states

Acting is doing, and acting is reacting; a character plays one action until something makes them play another (Hooks,
S7). Every performance starts with a verb in the present tense — what the body is doing in this instant.

| State (never) | Verb (always) |
|---|---|
| SON is sad | SON pulls his knees up and drops his forehead onto them |
| MOM is worried | MOM stops wiping the counter, cloth still in her mitten, and turns her head toward the stairs |
| SON is angry | SON shoves his chair back and stands |
| MOM is listening | MOM turns her phone face-down without looking at it and turns her shoulders to him |
| SON is embarrassed | SON rubs the back of his neck and looks at the floor |
| MOM is shocked | MOM freezes mid-step, one foot still lifted, the bag slipping from her mitten |
| SON doesn't care | SON keeps scrolling, thumb moving, while his pupils slide toward MOM |
| MOM is calm | MOM lowers herself onto the bottom stair, palms resting open on her knees |
| DAD is proud | DAD straightens up and brings his mittens together in one clap |

Stillness is a verb too: *freezes, stops, waits, holds back, hesitates, doesn't answer*. A held still is a choice
the character makes, so write it as one.

Words to keep out of every performance: garment words (hood, sleeve, pocket, shoe — the characters wear nothing but
their ROLE accessory, and the generator will draw the garment); anatomy words for teenagers (thigh, hip, waist, chest
— use geometry: shoulder height, leg, side); "hands on hips" (write "arms akimbo").

## 4. How to write a performance line

Lead with the name and the verb. Then the seven features in Thomas's order, each as mechanics. Every visible
character gets their own full line.

Pattern:
> NAME — *verb clause*: EYES …; EYEBROWS …; MOUTH …; HEAD …; HANDS …; POSTURE …; EYE DIRECTION ….

Example (a CLOSE on SON, MOM out of frame left):
> SON — pulls back from the question: EYES wide, white showing all round the pupils; EYEBROWS lifted high and pinched
> together; MOUTH a tight down-turned arc; HEAD pulled back and turned a quarter away; HANDS both mittens pressing
> the phone flat against his knee; POSTURE leaning back on the bed edge, shoulders up toward his ears; EYE DIRECTION
> pupils pressed to the corner toward MOM, out of frame left.

Example (a two-person MEDIUM; the second line is the reaction):
> MOM — sets her mug down and sits beside him: EYES soft, lids relaxed; EYEBROWS level, inner ends lifted a little;
> MOUTH closed in a small level line; HEAD tilted toward him; HANDS palms resting open on her knees; POSTURE leaning a
> few degrees toward him, shoulders loose; EYE DIRECTION on SON's face.
> SON — doesn't look up, but stops picking at the cable: EYES heavy; EYEBROWS inner ends up; MOUTH a pressed wavy
> line; HEAD bowed; HANDS still, the cable loose between his mittens; POSTURE hunched forward, shoulders starting to
> come down; EYE DIRECTION on the floor, then (edit) sliding toward MOM.

Rules:
- Back-turned or cropped characters still get head, hands and posture; name the face as hidden ("face turned away").
  In a HANDS frame the line describes the hands and forearms only.
- The emotion word may follow the mechanics once as an anchor ("…, reading as guarded"). It never replaces them.
- Make the strongest feature for this emotion carry the frame (for shame: posture, head and eyes down) and let the
  others support it.
- Keep it concrete. Words that do not draw anything ("very", "deeply", "intense") add nothing.
- Vary the wording across the film. The last video repeated the same few mouth phrases; the precise emotion of each
  beat gives a precise, different shape.

## 5. Expression seed bank

Seeds, not a menu. Each gives the verb, the seven features and where it fits. Adjust every seed to the precise cause,
target and shot, and build new ones with §6.

**Hesitation** (before telling the truth, before knocking) — *stops halfway*. Eyes open, a little wide. Brows inner
ends lifted, slightly pinched. Mouth half-open, caught before the first word. Head tipped down, chin drawn in. Hands one
mitten raised and stopped short of the door, the arm or the paper behind his back. Posture weight on the back foot,
the body leaning away while the hand still reaches. Eye direction on the target, then (edit) dropping to the floor.

**Pretending not to care** — *keeps doing his own thing*. Eyes heavy, nearly closed. Brows level and flat. Mouth a flat
line pulled slightly to one side. Head turned away, chin up a little. Hands busy — scrolling, spinning a ball, picking
at a cable. Posture weight on one leg or slouched back, body turned away. Eye direction the giveaway: pupils slid hard
toward the parent. (The face is the mask; the eyes betray it.)

**Looking away but listening** — *turns away, keeps listening*. Eyes normal, alert. Brows level, one slightly lifted.
Mouth closed, small and still. Head turned a little back toward the speaker over the shoulder. Hands slowing or
stopping on the task. Posture torso and shoulders turned away. Eye direction sliding back toward the speaker. An edit
can stop the hands on the word that matters.

**Noticing discomfort** (the one who notices) — *stops and looks again*. Eyes widening slightly. Brows inner ends
lifting, gap narrowing. Mouth closing mid-sentence into a small soft line. Head tilting, turning toward the other
person. Hands stopping mid-task (the cloth, the phone, the keys). Posture the whole movement halted, then a small
turn toward them. Eye direction fixed on the small sign: the untouched plate, the shoulders up, the thumb picking at a
case.

**Shame** — *makes himself small*. Eyes down, lids heavy. Brows inner ends up, outer ends dropped. Mouth a
wavering down-turn. Head bowed, chin tucked hard. Hands both mittens covering the face, or arms wrapped round the
knees. Posture curled small, shoulders up, back curved. Eye direction the floor, away from everyone.

**Overwhelm** — *sinks under it*. Eyes wide, unfocused. Brows inner ends up and pinched. Mouth a wavering open line.
Head sinking between the shoulders. Hands both mittens pressed to the head, or clutching too many things at once.
Posture hunched, knees bending, the body getting smaller. Eye direction darting — one pupil target, then another (two
stills).

**Relief** — *lets go*. Eyes shut, or soft and nearly closed. Brows relaxing down and level. Mouth a long breath out
through a small open shape, then a small curve. Head tipping back or dropping forward. Hands opening, loosening their
grip. Posture shoulders dropping, the body line lengthening. Eye direction closed, or soft on the person who caused
it.

**Pride (parent)** — *straightens and watches*. Eyes fully open, soft. Brows level, lifted slightly. Mouth a clean,
symmetrical smile, no teeth. Head lifted, tilted toward the child. Hands together at the top of the body line, or one mitten
resting on the child's shoulder. Posture upright, top of the body line straight. Eye direction on the child, not the
achievement.

**Quiet pride (teen)** — *tries not to smile*. Eyes bright. Brows lifted a little. Mouth a smile pressed down at one
corner. Head ducking slightly. Hands rubbing the back of the neck. Posture shifting weight from foot to foot. Eye
direction down, then a quick glance at the parent.

**Irritation** — *has had enough*. Eyes narrowed with a straight lid line. Brows pressed low, inner ends down. Mouth a
wide flat pressed line. Head turned a little away, chin set. Hands arms akimbo, or one mitten pressed to the forehead.
Posture still, braced, weight back. Eye direction slid sideways at the cause.

**The "seriously?" look** — *gives one slow look*. Eyes one narrowed with a straight lid line, one open. Brows one
lifted high, the other level. Mouth a flat line, one corner pulled in. Head tilted and pulled back a fraction. Hands
still — holding whatever they held, or arms crossed. Posture completely still (the stillness is the joke). Eye
direction straight at the person who caused it.

**The eye roll** — *rolls his eyes*. Eyes open. Brows lifted. Mouth a flat line. Head tipping back and to one side.
Hands dropping limp, or one flicking away. Posture weight on the back foot, turning away. Eye direction pupils pressed
to the top of the eye circles.

**"I'm fine" (forced brightness)** — *smiles too hard*. Eyes wide and too still. Brows lifted, inner ends pinched. Mouth
a big closed smile, too wide. Head nodding, tilted. Hands busy with something too hard (scrubbing a clean pan, stacking
plates). Posture stiff, upright. Eye direction not quite on the other person. The edit: the smile drops for one still
when she turns away.

**Hurt (stung)** — *flinches*. Eyes wide, then lowering. Brows inner ends up. Mouth a down-turn, opening slightly.
Head pulled back, then tipping down. Hands a hand that was reaching drops. Posture shoulders collapsing inward. Eye
direction on the person, then the floor.

**Giving up (being ignored)** — *lowers his hand*. Eyes on the parent, lids lowering. Brows lifted, then falling. Mouth
half-open, closing. Head dropping slightly. Hands the hand raised to speak or holding up a drawing lowers. Posture
energy draining, shoulders falling, turning to go. Eye direction from the parent to the floor.

**Guarded / defensive** — *closes up*. Eyes narrowed slightly. Brows drawn together. Mouth tight and small. Head pulled
back, chin tucked. Hands arms crossed tight, or the phone held against the body. Posture half-turned, one shoulder
forward like a shield. Eye direction sideways glance at the other person.

**Suspicion (parent)** — *leans in, slowly*. Eyes one narrowed with a lid line. Brows low and level, one slightly up.
Mouth a flat line pushed to one side. Head pushed forward a little, tilted. Hands holding still the thing she
noticed. Posture leaning forward. Eye direction on the hidden thing, not the face.

**Overthinking (parent)** — *spirals*. Eyes wide, pupils off to one side, seeing something that isn't there. Brows
pinched, one higher. Mouth a "o" or a pressed wavy line. Head tilted, thrust forward. Hands frozen mid-task —
the phone half-raised to call, the cup halfway. Posture stiff, leaning. Eye direction into the distance or at the tiny
trigger.

**Exaggerated parent reaction** — *snaps to attention*. Eyes wide open, white all round. Brows shooting up. Mouth a
big open oval. Head snapped up and pulled back. Hands both up, or one flat on the top of the body line. Posture frozen,
straight up. Eye direction locked on the trigger. Best as a large face on white (no cartoon effects).

**Calm attention (listening parent)** — *takes it in*. Eyes soft, steady. Brows level, relaxed. Mouth closed, a small
level line. Head tilted toward the speaker, a small nod (an edit). Hands still and open, resting. Posture turned fully
toward the speaker, leaning in slightly, level with them. Eye direction on the speaker's face.

**Tenderness** — *softens*. Eyes soft, lids relaxed. Brows level, inner ends lifted a little. Mouth a soft curve.
Head tilted. Hands a mitten resting lightly on a shoulder, or reaching to straighten something. Posture leaning in.
Eye direction on the other person.

**Worry (parent)** — *hovers*. Eyes open. Brows inner ends high, pinched. Mouth a tense down-turn. Head turned
toward the closed door or the stairs. Hands close to the top of the body line, holding something tight (the phone, a cloth).
Posture leaning toward the door without moving. Eye direction on the door, the phone or the clock.

**Bracing for the reaction (teen)** — *waits for the blow*. Eyes squeezing half shut. Brows inner ends up. Mouth a
tight pressed line. Head pulled down between the shoulders. Hands holding out the thing (the test, the cracked phone)
at arm's length. Posture leaning away, shoulders high. Eye direction checking the parent's face.

**Caught (guilt)** — *freezes*. Eyes wide. Brows lifted. Mouth a round "o". Head turning slowly toward the
parent. Hands one mitten hiding the thing behind his back. Posture frozen mid-movement. Eye direction on the parent,
then darting away.

**Embarrassment** — *cringes*. Eyes squeezed, or looking anywhere else. Brows lifted in the middle. Mouth an awkward
flat squiggle. Head ducking, sinking. Hands rubbing the back of the neck, or covering half the face. Posture shoulders
up, turning away. Eye direction down and to the side.

**Frustration (parent at the end of the rope)** — *rubs her forehead*. Eyes shut or narrowed. Brows knotted, inner
ends down. Mouth a pressed line, corners down. Head dropped forward. Hands one mitten pressed to the forehead.
Posture leaning on the counter, one shoulder turned. Eye direction closed, or away from the child.

**Anger held in** — *holds it*. Eyes narrowed, lid lines low. Brows pressed low, steep inward tilt. Mouth a wide flat
pressed line. Head very still, chin down. Hands fists at the sides, or gripping the chair back. Posture rigid,
squared. Eye direction locked on the cause.

**Anger let out** — *shouts*. Eyes wide, narrowed at the top by the lid line. Brows pressed low, steep. Mouth a wide
open oval, plain black inside. Head pushed forward. Hands the whole mitten aimed, or slapping the table. Posture
leaning forward, squared up, taking space. Eye direction on the other person.

**Realisation** — *stops*. Eyes widening slowly. Brows rising. Mouth slowly opening. Head pulling back a fraction.
Hands freezing mid-action. Posture straightening. Eye direction fixed on the thing or the person that made it land.

**Regret (just said the wrong thing)** — *wishes she could take it back*. Eyes widening, then lowering. Brows inner
ends up. Mouth an open shape, closing. Head pulling back. Hands one mitten rising toward the mouth. Posture
deflating. Eye direction on the hurt face, then away.

**Defiance** — *stands his ground*. Eyes narrowed. Brows low and level. Mouth a tight flat line. Head chin up. Hands
arms crossed. Posture squared, weight planted on both feet. Eye direction holding the parent's gaze.

**Withdrawal (sulking)** — *turns to the wall*. Eyes heavy. Brows inner ends slightly up. Mouth a pushed-out down-turn.
Head turned away. Hands hugging a cushion or the knees. Posture back turned, curled on the bed. Eye direction the
wall.

**Loneliness** — *sits apart*. Eyes soft, unfocused. Brows inner ends up a little. Mouth a flat line. Head
lowered, turned toward where the others are. Hands still, holding nothing. Posture small in a large empty frame,
sitting. Eye direction toward the others, who are not looking.

**Exhaustion (parent at night)** — *sags*. Eyes heavy, lids low. Brows outer ends drooping. Mouth slack and flat.
Head leaning back against the wall or dropping. Hands hanging loose (the one time hanging arms are right). Posture
slumped onto a chair or against the counter. Eye direction half-down, at nothing.

**Boredom (teen at the table)** — *drifts*. Eyes heavy. Brows flat. Mouth a flat line pushed to one side. Head cheek
propped on one mitten, elbow on the table. Hands the other mitten pushing food round the plate. Posture slumped
sideways. Eye direction out of the window, or at the phone under the table.

**Pleasant surprise** — *softens in surprise*. Eyes opening wider. Brows lifting, gap widening. Mouth an open "o"
turning into a curve. Head tilting. Hands pausing, then reaching for the thing offered. Posture turning toward. Eye
direction on the gesture, then the person.

**Shared laugh** — *laughs with her*. Eyes shut in curved lines. Brows lifted. Mouth a big open laugh, no teeth. Head
tipping back, or toward the other person. Hands one on the table, one on the other's arm. Posture leaning in. Eye
direction both on the same funny thing, or on each other.

**Opening up (trust returning)** — *turns toward her*. Eyes lifting from the floor. Brows relaxing, inner ends slightly
up. Mouth opening to speak. Head lifting and turning. Hands loosening, putting down what they held. Posture shoulders
dropping, body turning toward. Eye direction finding the parent's eyes.

**Apology (parent going first)** — *comes down to his level*. Eyes soft, steady. Brows inner ends up. Mouth a
open shape, mid-sentence. Head tilted. Hands palms open, low. Posture crouching or sitting so their heads are level.
Eye direction on his face, waiting for his eyes.

**Waiting (letting the silence sit)** — *waits*. Eyes steady, soft. Brows level. Mouth closed. Head still. Hands
resting, not reaching. Posture settled, not leaving. Eye direction on the other person or on the shared view.

**Disbelief** — *stares*. Eyes wide, perfectly still. Brows lifted high. Mouth dropped open in a long oval. Head pulled
back. Hands frozen, whatever they held hanging. Posture rigid. Eye direction locked on the impossible thing.

**Comparison sting** — *looks at the other one*. Eyes lowering. Brows inner ends lifting. Mouth a pressed line.
Head turning away from the parent. Hands a grip tightening on his own thing. Posture shrinking a little. Eye
direction at the sibling or cousin being praised.

**Cautious hope** — *looks up*. Eyes lifting. Brows lifted in the middle. Mouth a very small curve starting. Head
lifting. Hands loosening. Posture straightening slightly. Eye direction toward the parent, checking.

**Resignation ("whatever")** — *shrugs*. Eyes heavy. Brows flat. Mouth a flat line. Head a small sideways drop. Hands
both shoulders up and down in a shrug (an edit), palms turning up. Posture turning to leave. Eye direction away.

**Panic after an accident (the spill, the crack)** — *freezes over it*. Eyes wide, white all round. Brows high. Mouth a
small round "o". Head turned to the parent. Hands frozen in the air over the mess. Posture locked mid-movement. Eye
direction checking the parent's face before anything else.

## 6. Method: build any new expression

1. **Name the emotion precisely** — who feels it, about what, toward whom ("embarrassed that MOM saw the message on
   his screen").
2. **Find the verb** — what the body does in this instant (pulls back, freezes, leans in, hides, reaches, turns away,
   waits).
3. **Set the energy** — up and open (surprise, anger, joy) or down and closed (shame, hurt, sadness); toward the other
   person or away.
4. **Build the face from the dials** — brows (height, tilt, gap, asymmetry); eyes (open, lid line, shut); mouth (open or
   closed, curve, width, asymmetry, mid-change).
5. **Place the head** — tilt, chin, turn, pulled back or pushed forward.
6. **Give the hands one dominant gesture** — away from the body, readable as a silhouette.
7. **Shape the posture** — contracted or expansive, lean, weight, shoulders.
8. **Aim the eyes** — a target and a side that match the geography and the `look` field.
9. **Check one emotion** — if two feelings compete, split them across stills or across two named channels (§2).
10. **Push one notch past life** — inside the ceiling (§1.3).
11. **Check the shot** — small face → the body carries it; close → the eyes carry it.
12. **Write the performance line** (§4). After the video, add the new seed to §5.

## 7. Interaction — every frame with more than one person

Thomas asked for "more real interaction between characters". Every frame with more than one person shows **an action and a
visible reaction**. The plan records it in `ia` ("who does what → who reacts how"), with `dist` and, for close shots,
`look` (`shot-plan-v19.md`).

### 7.1 Action and reaction
- One character does something with their body, their voice or an object. The other visibly reacts in the same frame
  — or in the next still, when the reaction is the payoff.
- The reaction is a full second performance, usually a different emotion. It is often the stronger face. The cut to a
  reacting face tells the viewer what the moment means (Kuleshov, S4), so when the reaction is the point, favour the
  reacting face (a REACTION shot).
- When the point is that nobody reacts, the non-reaction is drawn as a choice and named in `ia`: "SON holds up the
  drawing → MOM's eyes stay on her phone (no reaction is the point)". His hand dropping is then the visible reaction.
- A watcher is never just "watching". Watching is a performance with a verb (bracing, hoping, checking, judging).

### 7.2 Eye contact — always name it
- **Met**: both pupils on each other, a clear straight line of sight between the faces.
- **Refused**: one looks, the other looks away.
- **One-way**: one watches; the other does not know.
- **Shared attention**: both look at the same thing (the view, the cake, the test).
- **Broken**: one looks away — an edit between two stills.
- **Sought**: one keeps trying to catch the other's eye.
Eye contact is story. It is usually avoided in conflict, sought in repair and met at the payoff.

### 7.3 Distance — a planned story variable
Every multi-person frame names one of four distances in `dist`. They follow Hall's zones (S11) and read the same way
on screen whatever the room (S12):

| `dist` | Zone | Reads as | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| touching | intimate | closeness, comfort, or control when unwanted | the payoff, comfort, a hand on the shoulder |
| close | personal (within reach) | family, friends, trust | repair, listening, sitting together |
| apart | social (across the room, across the table) | disconnection, formality, tension | conflict, misreading, the cold dinner |
| far | public (different ends of a wide frame, through a doorway) | separation, loneliness | after the argument, the closed door, years passing |

- Plan distance across the scene and the film like the colour script — a **distance script**: apart or far while
  things go wrong, close at the repair, touching where it pays off.
- A change of distance is an event. Stepping closer, sliding along the sofa, moving away to the far end of the table —
  each is an image edit or a new still.
- Distance can be shown without a room. Two small figures far apart in a CLEAN WIDE frame (one ground line, nothing else) is emotional distance, even
  "in the same kitchen". A tight two-shot where both heads nearly touch is closeness — no symbol needed.
- Write what the distance means, not a stock phrase: "apart: the whole table between them, neither leaning in", not
  "an arm's length away".

### 7.4 Power and orientation
Four dials for each person in the frame (immediacy behaviours, S13):
- **Lean**: forward, upright or back.
- **Orientation**: turned toward, half-turned, turned away, back turned.
- **Openness**: open (palms visible, arms away from the body) or closed (arms crossed, hands hidden, object held as a
  shield).
- **Level and space**: standing over, at the same eye height, or below; taking space (expansive) or making oneself
  small (contracted).

How the combinations read:

| Combination | Reads as | Parent–teen example |
|---|---|---|
| forward + closed | pressure, interrogation, aggression | "too many questions": MOM leaning over the table, the whole mitten aimed |
| forward + open | support, interest | MOM at his level, palms up, leaning in |
| back + open | cautious interest | SON leaning back on the bed but turned toward her, hands loose |
| back + closed | dismissal, defence | SON leaning back, arms crossed, turned away; or MOM misreading silence, arms akimbo |
| contracted | anxiety, shame, discomfort | SON small on the stairs, knees up |
| expansive | confidence, power, anger | DAD filling the doorway, arms out on the frame |
| same eye height | equality, safety | "a parent kneeling down to the child's level" |

A two-person scene is a negotiation of status (Hooks, S7): who stands over whom, who turns away, who takes up space,
who gives it up. Let the status change across a scene. Keep the camera at a natural eye level — exaggerated angles
render badly — and show status through height and posture inside the frame.

### 7.5 Touch
Touch is a story event. Build toward it with distance closing first, and place it where it pays off: comfort, repair,
the ending. Draw it simply: a mitten resting on the other's shoulder point, two mittens meeting on a table, shoulders
touching on a step. Unwanted touch (a hand on the shoulder he shrugs off) is also an interaction with a clear
reaction.

### 7.6 Silence
Silence is acting, not dead time — the pause filled with emotion (Hooks's "ma", S7). An awkward silence is drawn:
both mouths closed, eyes on different things, bodies angled apart, an empty space between them, nobody moving. A
held, loaded silence is a valid `ia`: "MOM waits → SON doesn't answer, eyes on his plate, spoon stopped in the air".

### 7.7 Staging two people without the same layout every time
The last video often put one character left, one right, an object between them. Inside one scene, characters keep
their screen sides (`camera-and-closeups-v19.md`); between scenes, vary the layout:
- over the shoulder — one back and shoulder in the foreground, the other face behind it;
- front and back — one near the camera, the other deeper in the frame;
- side by side facing the same way — on a step, in the car, on the sofa;
- one standing, one sitting; one in the doorway, one inside the room;
- back to back through a closed door (a cutaway);
- a tight two-shot, faces close;
- one face and the other's hand (FACE_HANDS);
- a REACTION shot with only the edge of the other's shoulder in frame.

### 7.8 Interaction seeds
Each seed gives the `ia` and a `dist`. Seeds, not a menu.
- **The hand-over refused** — MOM holds out her hand for the phone → SON pulls it against himself, eyes refusing hers.
  close.
- **Talked over** — SON starts to speak, mouth open, mitten raised → MOM is already talking, mouth wide, whole mitten
  aimed at him; (edit) his hand lowers. apart.
- **The doorway** — MOM leans in the doorway, one mitten on the frame → SON on the bed does not turn round; his
  shoulders rise. far.
- **The slide across the table** — DAD slides a plate of toast across → SON's eyes go to the plate, not to DAD; one
  mitten edges toward it. apart, the plate bridging it.
- **The tug** — both mittens on one object (the remote, the car keys) → both leaning back, eyes locked. close.
- **The follow** — MOM follows SON down the hall mid-question → SON keeps walking, head down, not turning. apart, both
  moving.
- **The mirror** — MOM reads a bill, shoulders rising → across the room SON's spoon stops and his shoulders rise to the
  same height (R2 S75). apart, no eye contact; the copy is the interaction.
- **The block** — SON stands in his doorway, one arm across the frame → MOM stops mid-step. close.
- **The check-back** — MOM walks past the open door → stops, leans back into the doorway, eyes on him. far → apart.
- **The weather check** — SON pauses at the kitchen door with the test → his eyes check MOM's face before he steps in.
  far.
- **The non-answer** — MOM asks → SON's eyes stay on his cereal; the spoon stops halfway (the stop is his reaction).
  apart.
- **The pull-away** — MOM's mitten reaches toward his shoulder → he shifts out of reach, a small lean away. close →
  apart.
- **The accepted touch** — MOM's mitten rests on his shoulder → his shoulders drop; he doesn't look up, but leans a
  fraction toward her. touching.
- **The shared laugh** — both look at the lopsided cake → both laugh, heads tipping toward each other. close.
- **Siblings** — the older sister takes the last slice → the younger brother's mouth drops open, mitten still reaching
  for the empty plate. close.
- **Friends** — a friend shows SON his phone screen → SON laughs, then glances at MOM to check she isn't looking.
  close (friend), far (MOM).
- **Grandparent** — GRANDMA holds out a biscuit tin without a word → SON, mid-sulk, takes one without looking at her;
  she smiles at the side of his face. close.

### 7.9 Method: invent a new interaction
1. What is the relationship doing in this line — pulling apart, testing, repairing, ignoring, protecting, competing,
   caring in silence?
2. Who starts it, and what do they do with their body or an object (the action)?
3. How does the other react — accept, refuse, freeze, mirror, ignore, return? Pick the reaction that carries the
   line's meaning.
4. Pick the distance class, and decide whether it changes in this beat.
5. Pick the eye-contact state.
6. Set each person's lean, orientation, openness and level.
7. Decide whether touch or an object bridges the gap. An object carries the behaviour; it never stands in for it.
8. Check: readable as silhouettes; one strong gesture each; revealed props apart from hands; the shot favours the
   reaction when the reaction is the point.
9. Write `ia`, `dist` and (for close shots) `look`. Add the new seed to §7.8 after the video.

## 8. Love, trust, attention and safety — staged through behaviour

**The rule:** a line about love, trust, attention or emotional safety is staged as a behaviour first. A metaphor is
used only if it adds meaning and passes the maturity test (`props-symbols-metaphors-v19.md`). Never a heart, a glow, a
sparkle or a symbol. Thomas: "If we want to show love, attention, or connection, it should come more through real
situations, behavior, closeness, eye contact, gestures, and reactions, not through simple standard symbols." The
summary he approved: "Love, trust and attention will come from behaviour: a parent sitting next to the teen, a hand
reaching out, eye contact, hesitation, distance between people, a parent pausing before reacting."

### 8.1 Thomas's list, staged
Each entry: what it carries → how to stage it → the change that moves it.

- **"a parent quietly sitting next to a teenager"** — presence without pressure; attention; safety. SON on the bed
  edge, the bottom stair or the back step. MOM sits down beside him, both facing the same way, nobody talking. MOM:
  palms open on her knees, head tilted toward him, a small level mouth, leaning a few degrees toward him. SON: eyes on
  the floor, hands loose, shoulders still up. `dist` close. Edit: his shoulders drop. A WIDE white frame with the two
  of them small on the step can carry the quiet; a CLOSE on his shoulders carries the change.
- **"a teen looking away but still listening"** — the teen hears more than he shows. Torso and shoulders turned away,
  head turned a little back, pupils sliding toward the parent, hands busy (the phone, a ball, a cable). The parent
  speaks calmly from the doorway or a chair. Edit: his hands stop on the word that matters.
- **"a hand reaching out"** — an offer. The reach is the action; the reaction decides the meaning (taken, ignored,
  stopped short). MOM's mitten reaching across the table toward his; the gap between the two mittens is the frame
  (HANDS or FACE_HANDS). Edit: his mitten moves to meet it — or stays.
- **"a parent stopping before reacting"** — self-control; safety to make mistakes. Setup: the trigger (the broken glass,
  the bad test, the late arrival). Beat: MOM frozen with her mouth already open and one hand up. Reaction: the hand
  lowers, the shoulders drop with a breath out, the brows come down from alarm to level. A REACTION or CLOSE on her
  face, with the trigger established before.
- **"a child hesitating before telling the truth"** — fear of the reaction; trust being tested. SON at the kitchen door,
  the test behind his back, one foot forward, one back, a mitten on the door frame, mouth half-open, eyes on MOM's
  back. Edit: he steps in — or steps back.
- **"someone noticing the other person is uncomfortable"** — attention that sees. The noticer's eyes stop on a small sign
  (the untouched plate, the thumb picking at the phone case, the raised shoulders); their own movement stops; brows lift
  in the middle; head tilts. The other person does not know yet. Over the shoulder, or a CLOSE on the noticer with the
  cause in frame or just established (`ctx`).
- **"a parent kneeling down to the child's level"** — respect; safety; no power gap. MOM drops to one knee so the two
  heads are at the same height in frame, palms open, face level. The child's shoulders come down. Camera at the
  child's eye height.
- **"two people sitting together without needing a heart symbol"** — connection without words. Side by side, both looking
  at the same thing (the view from the car, the rain at the window, the TV), shoulders close or touching, nothing said.
  Shot from behind (two heads, two backs) or from the front; a WIDE frame with a lot of white around them.

### 8.2 More seeds of the same kind
Each is a behaviour a parent recognises from their own home. Seeds, not a menu.
- Turning the phone face-down the moment the child starts talking — attention.
- Turning the whole body toward the child, not just the head — attention.
- Sitting on the landing floor outside a closed door, not knocking — patience; safety.
- A plate of food left outside the door after an argument, one knock, footsteps away — care after conflict.
- Keeping the engine off and staying in the parked car when the teen doesn't get out — space to talk side by side.
- The teen coming back to the kitchen "for water" again, hovering in the doorway — wanting connection.
- The teen sitting at the far end of the sofa, then (edit) a little closer — trust growing.
- The teen leaning a head on the parent's shoulder for a moment while both watch TV — trust.
- Letting the teen finish the sentence: mouth closed, a small nod, no interruption — listening.
- Handing over the car keys or the house key — trust.
- The parent saying sorry first, sitting so their heads are level, palms open — repair.
- Laughing together at the parent's own mistake (the keys found in the fridge) — safety to fail.
- The teen showing the parent his phone screen without being asked — trust.
- The parent lowering their voice and sitting down when the teen goes quiet — attunement.
- A hand hovering over the door handle, then lowering — choosing not to barge in; respect for autonomy.
- A grandparent and child with their hands busy on the same task (peeling, folding, fixing a bike chain) — safe
  presence.
- A sibling sliding over to make room on the sofa — belonging.
- Picking up the dropped school bag without a comment — care without a lecture.
- The parent sends one short message from downstairs; the teen's phone lights up with a plain grey message bar (no
  words in the image) and his face softens — reaching out.
- Sitting on the floor by the bed in the dark, back against the bed frame, the child's hand hanging down within reach
  — safety at night.

### 8.3 Method: turn any love, trust, attention or safety line into a behaviour
1. Name which one the line is about, and what threatens it in this story.
2. Ask what a parent or teen does with their body, time, attention or an everyday object that shows it without words:
   sits, waits, stops, turns toward, puts down, comes back, leaves something, lowers to the other's level, lets them
   finish.
3. Pick the smallest real action a parent would recognise from their own home.
4. Give the other person a reaction that shows it landed — or not yet (shoulders drop, eyes slide back, a small lean).
5. Set distance and contact. Use an object only as the carrier of the behaviour (the mug set down beside him), never
   as a symbol.
6. Choose the shot that shows the key part: hands for a reach, a REACTION for the landing, WIDE white for the quiet.
7. Record `ia`, `dist` and `look`. Add the new behaviour to §8.2 after the video.

## 9. Where this goes in the plan and the prompt
- `ft` — what the viewer should feel (the reason for every choice here).
- Performance text — one line per visible character (§4).
- `ia` — "who does what → who reacts how" (required when more than one person is visible).
- `dist` — touching / close / apart / far.
- `look` — gaze target and side for CLOSE, XCLOSE, FACE_HANDS and REACTION frames with a face.
- In-scene changes (a blink, the eyes sliding back, the shoulders dropping, a step closer) are image-edit prompts on
  the generated frame — one change at a time, everything else unchanged.
