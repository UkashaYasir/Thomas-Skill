# Thomas's standards — what he set, why, and every complaint behind them

> HISTORY FILE: Thomas's feedback round by round, kept for the why behind each rule. Where an older
> round differs from the current rules, the current rules win — `v19-principles.md` first, then
> RULES-CARD.md and the top of SKILL.md. Since 5 Oct 2026: white or very light neutral backgrounds are the
> default, places are outlines, love and trust are shown through behaviour, the camera comes closer,
> there are no creative numbers, and there is no approval stop. The 5 Oct round is summarised at the end
> of this file; his exact words are in `v19-principles.md`.

Thomas reviews scene by scene, often with timestamps, and expects every point to become permanent:
*"Please treat these points not only as revisions for this video, but as a fixed standard for all
upcoming videos."* And since Video 3: *"All improvements must already be present in the first
version. Please do not add them only during revisions."* He rates on a 10-point scale (Video 1 draft
8.2; Video 2 8.5 before fixes) and says quality comes before speed — but he also needs reliable
delivery of two videos a week.

## The ten standards (after Video 1)
1. The first 1–3 seconds create immediate attention. No slow or neutral opening.
2. Emotions always clearly visible through facial expression, eyes, hands and body language.
3. Intentional close-ups during important emotional lines.
4. Visual metaphors instantly understandable — a detail too subtle loses its impact.
5. Strong red reserved for danger, warning or negative pressure; other props use other accents.
6. Teenagers and adults instantly distinguishable "through height, facial features, body proportions,
   clothing and other visual characteristics" (his words). Clothing stays minimal: the body and
   limbs are never clothed (no shirts, jackets, dresses, trousers), and characters may wear small
   accessories — a tie, a scarf, a cap, a beanie, glasses — locked in their ROLE. Proportions follow the
   approved MOM and SON reference images exactly (MOM's head about one-fifth of her height, SON's
   about one-quarter), never bigger than in the references. See `characters.md` and the recognition
   kit in `emotion-and-performance.md`.
7. The reduced style is the base, but important objects can and should be highlighted with colour.
8. No overly happy or perfect endings — relief, cautious closeness, a calm emotional ending.
9. No artificially long held shots or silence to pad runtime.
10. Every scene has a purpose: emotion, information, or maintaining attention.

## How to spend detail (after the Video 1 sample)
*"The sample is almost too perfect or too detailed in every single scene… normal explanation scenes
can be simpler and cleaner; emotional key moments should get the stronger close-ups, facial
expressions, eye direction, hands and body language; major hooks or pattern interrupts can use
stronger metaphors or more complex visuals… keep it simple where simple is enough, and add detail
where the emotion really matters."* That is the tier system, and it's why detail sits on the hero and
not on the setting.

## Seven more (after Video 2)
11. Every recurring object keeps its design: the guitar keeps its strings. Countable build specs.
12. Object consistency is as binding as character consistency.
13. Hands stay clean and consistent — sometimes they had hands, sometimes they partly didn't.
14. Important objects can suddenly become very large and dominate the frame (a scale field).
15. Faces can occupy 60–80% of the frame; expressions can be intentionally exaggerated (a face field).
16. Isolate the hands as a shot type.
17. Amplify the idea behind the sentence — without forcing a metaphor into every segment.

## Nine more (Video 3 brief)
18. Colours much more selective: never a whole scene in one dominant colour.
19. Every Video 2 improvement present in the first version.
20. Stronger symbolism from the script: obstacle, crossroads, race, balancing act, maze, dominoes.
21. More drama and action on important moments: something happens inside the scene.
22. Stronger emotions: fear, surprise, overwhelm, pride, happiness, frustration, realisation.
23. Visual progression inside a scene: start → problem or change → reaction → result.
24. Fewer basic "character + object + background" illustrations — the most interesting,
    unexpected way to tell the sentence.
25. Regular new visual information: close-ups, wide shots, perspective changes, movement, reactions,
    new locations alternating. (v19: the new information comes from camera distance, composition and
    contrast inside outline places — not from more rooms.)
26. Less unnecessary information inside each frame.

## Every complaint — what he said, what he wanted, what we were doing, what replaced it
Read the middle column hardest: he describes symptoms, and the fix is rarely the symptom.

**1 · "The faces are still too neutral."** (Video 1) *Wanted:* emotion readable from the face alone on
a paused frame. *We did:* calm default expressions. *Now:* expression table, pushed stronger than life,
a ceiling, never a blank close-up.

**2 · "He looks too young, almost like a small child."** *Wanted:* a teen reads 13–17 at a glance.
*Now:* same head ratio as the adult, leaner, longer limbs, shorter than the adult — head at her
shoulder; children a different build entirely.

**3 · "Show the process — he stopped trying to be heard."** *Wanted:* cause and effect, not a mood
shot. *Now:* process lines storyboarded as sequences.

**4 · "Avoid becoming an infographic channel."** Seven cards, many colours, small text; red X / green
check doing the work. *Wanted:* CHARACTER → EMOTION → ACTION → SYMBOL. *Now (v19):* no generic symbols;
emotion through behaviour; on-screen idea words only at strong moments, added in Premiere; no number
slides.

**5 · "The hug says love, not influence."** *Wanted:* the image says exactly what the line says. *Now:*
the idea-precision check in `idea-not-object.md`.

**6 · "The large 7 stays alone for six seconds."** *Wanted:* curiosity, not a title card. *Now (v19):*
no number card at all — chapter numbers are not on-screen words; go straight to a fast visual preview.

**7 · "Almost the same image appears again."** (the mother with the cup) *Wanted, his words:* change
the camera distance, the hand movement, the facial expression or the overall composition. *Now:*
recycled-shot rule and QA warning.

**7b · "The people and their emotional interaction must remain the main focus."** *Wanted:* the
relationship readable through expressions, hands, posture, eye direction, distance and interaction.
*Now:* action-and-reaction in every shared frame (`ia`), the distance planned (`dist`), each character
performed with all seven features, the eye line stated, an INTERACTION block compiled into every
multi-character prompt.

**8 · "The guitar changes between scenes. Strings disappear."** (Video 2) *Wanted:* finished,
professional. *We did:* prose object descriptions. *Now:* countable build specs and a build order.
*Generalise:* anything that must not drift needs a procedure and a count of its parts, never an
adjective (object build specs — a technical number, not a creative quota).

**9 · "Sometimes they have hands and sometimes they partially do not."** *Wanted:* simple ≠
inconsistent. *We did:* hand rule only in some beats, then "thumb notch" + "never fingers" together.
*Now:* one global hand lock, one positive definition, the loop closes before the object passes behind.

**10 · "Not enough close-ups. Faces can occupy 60–80%."** *Wanted:* emotion recognisable instantly for
8:30. *We did:* level eye-line, natural scale everywhere. *Now:* face and shot size as fields on every
frame, chosen by the moment, and every close-up planned with its gaze and context (`look`, `ctx`).
*Generalise:* anything he asks for by degree becomes a planned field with a reason, never a quota.

**11 · Our overcorrection: gritted teeth and rage faces.** *Now:* scale features, not distortion; a
stated ceiling every time.

**12 · "Similar rooms, similar furniture, similar perspectives."** *Wanted:* something new for the eye
every few seconds — a retention note, not a decorating note. *Then:* more locations, which became full
rooms. *Now (v19):* places are outline cues; variety is **how close**, **from what angle**, **which
composition** and **which contrast** — plus time moving.

**13 · "Don't always show the complete character sitting at a table."** *Now:* hands-only, face +
hands, object-only, large faces, reaction shots; a run in one place keeps changing size, angle and face
size.

**14 · "Visualise the emotion behind the sentence."** *Now:* his four questions and the noun-in-hands
count.

**15 · "The rule is not: every scene needs a metaphor."** *We did:* three invented backbone props
(cord, photographs, drawings) — all rejected as meaningless. *Now:* what holds a film together is time
moving forward, a character state that never resets, consequences you can point at. Metaphors on the
lines that earn them, one frame each.

**16 · "Red for danger."** *Now:* DANGER_RED on danger objects only; pick objects everyone knows (a red
correcting pen, a warning light).

**17 · Our failure: prompts stopped generating.** 22k characters opening with "READ THESE EIGHT RULES
FIRST… OVERRIDE". *Now:* ≤ 18k, zero instruction-shaped wording.

**18 · Our failure: the QA went green while the pictures got worse.** Fifteen rounds of adding rules
with no render in the loop; one prompt was mostly prohibitions. It happened again with Seven Things: the
suite passed while the rooms were built around one colour tone. *Now:* rules that don't fight, a prompt
that leads with the picture, checks that test v19 rules rather than shares, and render lessons fed back
into the rules.

**19 · "Some scenes almost completely one colour — a lot of brown, an entire green environment."**
(Video 2) *We did:* forced "three areas of solid colour" + environment hues matching prop hues. *Now:*
the hero hue sits far from the background hue; no quota language.

**20 · "The opening is still too calm; scenes feel illustrative."** (Video 3 sample) *Wanted:* tension
and energy in the first 20–30 seconds; things happening inside scenes. *Now:* the opening is
storyboarded as its own sequence; pop-ins dense there.

**21 · "Pan and zoom on static images is a little too limited."** *Wanted:* small visual surprises,
not full animation. *Now:* pop-in cues (ADD / PUNCH) with overlay prompts.

**22 · "Too much beige, pale green, grey and soft pastel… the eye doesn't know where to look."**
(Video 3 review) *We did:* our own "environment black / white / pale grey only" rule. *Then:* the
colour script, mood palettes with separated furniture, a saturated hero, peaks. He praised the orange
scene at 2:20 and the dark storm scene because each had its own mood. *Now (v19):* the same hues came
back as tinted rooms and he named them again on 5 Oct ("blue, beige, or green"); the answer is a white
or very light neutral ground with outline places, one strong colour element, and full colour only for
peaks and night.

## Video 4 review (Sept 2026) — the current direction
Thomas: characters, emotions, different scenes and close-ups are *much better — continue in this
direction*. Two areas to push, for this and all future videos:
1. **Less colour, used intentionally.** Orange/peach and other full-colour backgrounds appeared too
   often. *"70–80% neutral and clean, 20–30% targeted color accents"* — e.g. the phone strong turquoise
   while the room stays neutral. (v19 reads this per frame — most of the frame clean, colour on the key
   object — never as a share of frames.) The dark blue/grey night scenes work because the colour has a
   psychological purpose. *"Neutral first. Color with purpose. Never color just to fill empty space."*
2. **Bolder objects and visual ideas.** Standard objects (phone, table, sofa, bed, lamp, door, clock) feel
   repetitive. More unusual metaphors and surprising objects — pressure as a heavy backpack pulling the
   character down, lack of trust as a wall appearing between them, phone addiction as an oversized phone
   pulling the character in, time pressure as a giant clock, too many messages as a mountain of messages.
   *"The goal is not simply to illustrate what the voice-over says. We need to visually amplify the
   voice-over."* He asked for fresh visual stimulus roughly every 20–30 seconds, never chaotic — fewer
   elements can be stronger if they are more interesting and intentional. (v19 keeps the intent without
   a timer: the video never settles into one look.)

## 5 Oct 2026 — "Seven Things" review: the current direction (v19)
