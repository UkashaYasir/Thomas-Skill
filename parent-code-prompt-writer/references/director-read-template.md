# The director's read — the first deliverable of every video (fill this before the shot plan)

> **v19:** the director's read is the whole-film view; the per-frame plan that follows it is in `shot-plan-v19.md`.
> Thomas: "define the emotional beats, camera angles, reactions, and visual contrasts very clearly before production
> starts." No creative numbers here: every row is judged by the script.

**1. The film in one sentence** — what the viewer should understand and feel by the end.

**2. Emotional curve** — one row per chapter: chapter · what happens · what the viewer should feel · strength (quiet,
building, strong or peak) · build / peak / release. Mark the emotional peaks: only these may take a full-colour PEAK frame.

**3. Signature images** — the pictures people will remember: line · the image · why it explains the idea · where it pays
off later.

**4. Move or hold** — for each chapter: the lines where the story moves (an action, a turn, a punchline → an edit or a
sequence) and the lines where it must land (a statement, a hurt, an aha → a new image or a deliberate hold).

**5. Face, idea, moment or place** — for each chapter: where we must see a face (emotion is the information — bring the
camera closer), a real everyday moment (`everyday-situations-v19.md`), an idea (a metaphor only where it adds meaning) or
the place (an outline cue to orient the viewer before the close shots).

**6. Camera distance and reactions** — for each chapter: where the camera comes close (close-up, extreme close-up,
face-only, face + hands, reaction shot), where a wide frame places us, and the planned reaction for each key beat (who
reacts, how). No long stretch stays at the same medium/wide framing.

**7. Interaction and distance** — for each chapter: how the people's distance moves (apart for conflict, close for repair,
touch where it pays off) and where love, trust, attention or safety is shown through behaviour
(`acting-and-interaction-v19.md`).

**8. Contrast, interrupts, humour** — where each contrast lands (empty vs strong object, neutral vs one bright colour, wide
vs extreme close-up, serious vs humour, still vs movement, small prop vs large face, silence vs beat), where pattern
interrupts break the rhythm, and where the human, relatable humour sits (`humour-text-contrast-v19.md`).

**9. Colour** — white or very light neutral by default; the colour elements that matter in each chapter; the emotional
peaks that get a PEAK field; the night scenes (NIGHT); the past (MEMORY).

**10. Recurring objects** — each one's reason, how it evolves, and what it means each time it returns (it never returns
only because it looks good).

**11. On-screen words** — the idea words at the strong moments (TRUST, SAFE, LISTEN, SHAME, MISREAD, NOT REJECTION…),
never chapter numbers, not constantly.

**12. Risks** — the extremes Thomas would correct (rooms built around one colour, full rooms, symbols instead of behaviour,
the camera stuck at medium/wide, a repeated composition, too much text, too random, too literal) and how this video
avoids each.

Every frame later carries its plan fields (`ft`, `idea`, `alt`… — `shot-plan-v19.md`) that trace back to this read; the optional one-line `y` (why) note can add the reason in words.
