# The Parent Code system — what happens before any prompt is written (brought up to v19)

> **v19:** `v19-principles.md` is the law; this file is the order of work. The per-frame plan now lives in
> `shot-plan-v19.md` and is written before any prompt. There are no creative numbers in this file: every choice is judged
> by the moment.

Every step below is carried out through `director-framework.md` (the director's read, the questions, moment types).

History: proven on Video 05 ("The Answer Is Still No"), which Thomas accepted on 2 Oct 2026 after calling it "a clear
improvement … visual organization, character interaction, motion, environments and visual metaphors are much stronger
now. Please keep this direction." Then reshaped by his 5 Oct 2026 notes on "Seven Things" (v19).

## The goal (definition of done)
1. **Emotion before decoration.** For every frame the plan says what the viewer should feel (`ft`) before anything is
   decided about background, prop, colour, camera or movement.
2. The story never feels static: images move where the story moves (an edit, a sequence, a reveal, a camera move or a
   new image), and stillness is used on purpose where a moment should land.
3. Every chapter has strong ideas that explain it; a recurring object returns only when it means something new, evolves
   with the story and supports the emotional progression. Real everyday moments carry the everyday lines; humour arrives
   where the tone allows.
4. The video never settles into one look and no composition comes back unchanged; pattern interrupts land where attention
   would drop (`humour-text-contrast-v19.md`).
5. Every powerful line visually feels important — a closer camera, a face, a held beat or a sequence — and every close-up
   makes sense (`camera-and-closeups-v19.md`).
6. Every place reads at once from its outline cue; characters keep their screen sides inside a scene.
7. Faces are readable at phone size, and every performance shows eyes, eyebrows, mouth, head position, hand gesture,
   posture and eye direction (`acting-and-interaction-v19.md`).
8. Colour: white or very light neutral by default; one colour element per frame; full colour only for emotional peaks and
   night (`style-and-colour-v19.md`).
9. Text only as intentional on-screen idea words added in Premiere — never baked into the image.
10. The scorecard below reads strong on every row.

## The steps
**Director's read (first).** Read the whole script as a viewer: emotional curve, the signature images, where it moves and
where it stands still, where we need a face, an idea or the place (`director-framework.md` §1,
`director-read-template.md`).
**0 — Intake and research.** Final line-broken script; Thomas's notes; the previous video's ideas. Research the concept
(what it is, misconceptions, what parents and teens live through). Output: a research sheet of real everyday situations
(`everyday-situations-v19.md`) and idea candidates, with the clichés listed so they are avoided.
**1 — Story map and video spine.** Per chapter: the point in one sentence, the viewer's question, the feeling, the
powerful lines, the turn. For the video: character arcs; recurring objects, each with a reason and a new meaning on every
return; an intensity curve with clear peaks and a quiet held frame after each; the opening hook and an ending that calls
back to the opening.
**2 — Ideas.** For every line, explore every direction — the real everyday moment, the behaviour, a reaction, an object, a
contrast, humour, a metaphor and an unexpected angle — and keep going until one idea is clearly the strongest. Pick it;
record the runner-ups (`alt`) so later lines can use them and nothing repeats. Never the first, most obvious idea;
nothing reused unchanged from the previous video.
**3 — Scene cards.** Group lines into scenes (one place, continuous time, one visible change): Story (an everyday moment,
its place shown by an outline cue on the light ground) / Idea (white, or a metaphor where it adds meaning the words
don't carry) / Insert / Peak. Each card: place and its outline cue, cast, key object, start → end, sequence?, humour?,
mood (CLEAN, WHITE, PEAK, NIGHT, MEMORY).
**4 — Shot plan** (`shot-plan-v19.md`). Each line: what the viewer should feel (`ft`), the line type (`ln`), the chosen idea
and the runner-ups (`idea`, `alt`), the interaction beat and distance when two or more people are in frame (`ia`, `dist`),
gaze and context for close shots (`look`, `ctx`), the one colour element (`ce`), the contrast it makes (`cx`), the
interrupt type when it is one (`ip`), and `pk` on an emotional-peak frame. Then shot and angle with a reason, placement
(left · centre · right), action verb + object, what changed, what the picture adds.
**5 — Bibles.** Places (one outline cue each and a fixed layout), objects (shape, colour, structural details, what it does,
its journey and what it means each time it returns), cast (`cast-design-v19.md`), acting guide, composition tracker.
**6 — Sequences and transitions.** Short sequences of stills only for mini-stories with visible cause and effect at key
emotional or turning moments, not every scene; everywhere else the cut is the motion. Base = the fullest moment, earlier
states by removing, later by edits, never more than two edits away from a generated base (edits degrade the image).
Transitions: shape match, object carry, colour bleed, exit/enter, push into an object.
**7 — Prompts** (long, structured — see `render-lessons.md`).
**8 — Motion and sound plan.** Rhythm per scene; move per beat (push = tension, snap = punchline, pan = travel, tilt =
scale, hold = quiet); holds where the moment needs weight; no move type takes over; pop-ins with a bounce; match cuts;
sound cues; audio −14 LUFS.
**9 — Checks.** All scripts in `scripts/` (the v19 suite — see `assets/compiler-v24/README.md`): rule checks must pass;
printed distributions are information, never targets.
**10 — Learning loop.** Every note becomes a rule + a before/after from a real render + a check; rules that don't improve
renders are removed. New ideas, situations, behaviours and compositions that worked are added back to the v19 libraries.

## Delivery — one pass
There is no approval stop and no per-chapter render loop: the plan and the prompts are complete in one delivery, and
Muhammad generates the whole video from the Copy page in one pass (`v19-principles.md` §6). The plan is written into the
build so Muhammad can read it next to the frames.

## The creative pass is not optional
Video 05's biggest lesson: re-staging the old frames (camera, sides, sizes) while keeping their ideas reads to the client
as "nothing improved". Every chapter needs a creative pass on its ideas — real everyday moments, concept-explaining
metaphors where they add meaning, designed aha moments, real interaction (an action and a visible reaction) — not only a
staging pass. Distance between characters is planned: apart for conflict, close for repair, touch where it pays off.

## Scorecard (judge each row: weak, fair or strong)
Story reads without sound · fresh ideas throughout, nothing repeating · objects designed, used and mature · places
recognisable from their outline cues · faces readable on a phone and clearly reacting · a change every line · camera
distance varied with a reason · white by default, colour with meaning · humour human and relatable · key lines feel
important and memorable.
