# Everyday situations — real moments parents and teens recognise (v19)

Read with `v19-principles.md` §4. Use with `acting-and-interaction-v19.md` (how the people act),
`humour-text-contrast-v19.md` (when the moment is funny) and `shot-plan-v19.md` (where the choice is recorded).

**This is an open library.** Every situation below is a seed, plus a method (§4) for turning any script line into a
real moment. It is never a menu, and nothing here has to be used. When a line needs a moment that is not listed,
invent it with the method. After every video, add the situations that worked.

## 1. How a situation works on this channel

**The shape: setup → beat → reaction.**
- **Setup** — where we are and who is there, in as few lines as possible: one door outline, one table outline, one car
  seat. Often a wider frame.
- **Beat** — the one instant everyone recognises: the phone flipped face-down a little too fast, the hand stopping on
  the door handle, the plate left untouched. Usually a small action with a give-away detail.
- **Reaction** — the face or body that tells the viewer what it means: who sees it, and how they read (or misread) it.
  Often a CLOSE or REACTION shot.

**Length.** A situation can be one frame (the beat with the reaction inside it) when the line is short. An important
moment can be told as a short sequence of stills in one scene — not every scene. When the camera stays, the next still
is an image-edit prompt on the frame before (one change at a time). When the camera moves in for the reaction, it is a
new frame whose gaze matches the geography (`look`, `ctx`).

**The line stays fixed.** The voice-over is final. The situation plays under it. The picture can show what the line
does not say, or contradict it (that is where the humour lives).

**Production limits.**
- No words in the image. What people say is the voice-over; on screen, speech is a mouth shape. Phone screens show plain
  grey message bars or one simple drawn picture, never words. No speech or thought bubbles holding pictures.
- No garments. Nobody wears a hoodie, a coat or shoes; a bag is carried, not worn. (Write "the backpack hanging from his
  mitten", not "his backpack on".)
- Places are thin soft-grey outlines with white fill — one door, one table, one bed, one car seat — and the one colour
  element sits on the object that matters, or nothing carries colour and the face holds the frame
  (`style-and-colour-v19.md`).
- Props that appear on a word are revealed with a flat-shape mask, so they sit apart from hands and bodies.

**Every situation carries ideas.** Each seed lists the psychology ideas it can carry, so a line about that idea can
find a real moment instead of a symbol.

## 2. Thomas's situations, staged

Each one: setup → beat → reaction, the ideas it carries, and notes.

**Teen says "I'm fine" but clearly is not.**
- Setup: SON comes in through the front door outline, his bag hanging from one mitten.
- Beat: MOM asks how his day was. SON gives a big closed smile that is too wide and raises his thumb — and is already
  heading for the stairs, the other mitten gripping the bag strap hard.
- Reaction: the smile drops the moment his back is turned (an edit only the viewer sees), or MOM's eyes follow him, her
  brows lifting in the middle.
- Carries: masking; teens protecting their parents; the gap between words and body; why direct questions bounce off;
  emotional safety.
- Variants: the plate that goes back untouched; "fine" with the bedroom light still on at midnight. For the parent's
  own "I'm fine", see S82–S83 in §5.

**Parent asks too many questions.**
- Setup: SON drops onto a kitchen chair outline after school.
- Beat: a quick run of stills of MOM — leaning in further each time, the whole mitten aimed, mouth wide, a new question
  shape on each.
- Reaction: a dead-still CLOSE on SON's flat face, held. The fast run against the still face is the joke.
- Carries: interrogation vs curiosity; why teens shut down; anxiety-driven parenting; control; listening vs fixing.

**Teenager rolls their eyes.**
- Setup: MOM gives advice, or says something embarrassing in front of SON's friend.
- Beat: SON's pupils press to the top of the eye circles, his head tipping back and to one side.
- Reaction: either the exaggerated parent reaction (a mitten flat at the base of her neck, mouth open), or MOM stopping before
  reacting — she notices, breathes out and doesn't bite.
- Carries: autonomy; identity; testing limits; not taking it personally; respect vs control.

**Child hides something.**
- Setup: SON at the table; his phone lights up.
- Beat: he flips it face-down a beat too fast.
- Reaction: MOM's single raised eyebrow — or she misses it, and he lets out a breath.
- Carries: secrecy; shame; fear of the reaction; privacy vs safety; trust; the cost of harsh punishment.
- Variants: the test pushed deeper into the bag; the cracked phone kept in his lap under the table.

**Parent walks past the room, notices something is wrong, and comes back.**
- Setup: WIDE — MOM walks past SON's half-open door outline with a laundry basket.
- Beat: XCLOSE — her eyes slide back toward the door; she has stopped.
- Reaction: MEDIUM — MOM leans back into the doorway; SON, knees up on the bed, looks up.
- Carries: attunement; noticing; being available; small moments matter. Funny when the reason turns out to be tiny,
  tender when it is not. This is the "there and back again" move (R3 §5).

**Teen wants attention but pretends not to care.**
- Setup: MOM on the sofa outline with a laptop.
- Beat: SON hovers in the doorway "looking for snacks" — opens the fridge, closes it, drifts closer, steals glances.
- Reaction: MOM looks up; he looks away at once and studies the cereal box. Edit: he sits on the far arm of the sofa.
- Carries: teens still need their parents; bids for connection; independence vs closeness; ambivalence.

**Parent misunderstands silence as disrespect.**
- Setup: MOM asks SON something at the table.
- Beat: SON goes quiet and looks down — he is thinking, or overwhelmed.
- Reaction: MOM, arms akimbo, brows pressed down, mouth open mid-"Don't give me that look". A cut to SON's large face on
  white shows what is really there (worry, hurt). What she sees against what is inside is the point.
- Carries: misreading; processing time; shame; the behaviour vs the feeling underneath. A strong place for the word
  MISREAD.

**Child starts to say something but changes their mind.**
- Setup: SON at the table, or in the kitchen doorway, takes a breath.
- Beat: his mouth opens and one mitten lifts.
- Reaction: MOM looks up — SON's mouth closes, "…never mind", and he turns back to his cereal. MOM's face: she knows
  she missed something.
- Carries: hesitation; timing; safety to speak; what past reactions taught him.

**Parent tries to help but accidentally makes the situation worse.**
- Setup: SON stuck on his homework, frustrated.
- Beat: MOM leans over, takes the pencil and starts writing the answer — or picks up her phone to call the teacher while
  SON's palms go up in protest.
- Reaction: SON pushes back from the table, arms crossed; MOM, pencil still in her mitten, confused.
- Carries: rescuing vs supporting; autonomy; competence; "listen, don't fix".

**Awkward silence after an argument.**
- Setup: WIDE on white, one table outline, MOM and DAD at opposite ends.
- Beat: DAD reaches for the salt; MOM slides it over without looking up.
- Reaction: SON's pupils go left, then right (an edit).
- Carries: repair; tension in the home; children reading the atmosphere; parents' conflict and the child.
- Variant: MOM and SON after a fight both reach for the kettle at once — and both pull back.

## 3. Seed situations across ages and places

Seeds, not a menu. Each is setup → beat → reaction, then the ideas it can carry. Ages run from young children to young
adults; change the age and place to fit the script. The outline cue for each place is enough to set it.

### Mornings (outline cues: the bedroom door, the kitchen counter, the front door)
- **The snooze** — dark bedroom, phone alarm glowing → one mitten comes out from under the duvet and taps snooze →
  MOM back at the door again, brows up, arms akimbo. *Teen sleep; the nagging cycle; natural consequences.*
- **The lunchbox left behind** — the front door closes → the lunchbox alone on the counter (OBJECT) → MOM sees it, reaches
  for her keys, then puts them down. *Rescuing vs responsibility; letting them learn.*
- **The spoon refused** (young child) — breakfast table → the spoon offered, the child turns her head away, mouth
  pressed shut → MOM's spoon hovering, eyes closing for a breath. *Autonomy in young children; picking battles.*
- **The rush** — MOM at the front door, keys in her mitten, eyes on the oven clock → SON drifting down the stairs, eyes
  on his phone → MOM's free mitten opening in a silent "seriously?". *Stress passed down; time pressure.*

### Home (the hall, the stairs, the landing, the bedroom door)
- **The knock that doesn't happen** — SON's door closes → MOM's mitten raised to knock stops → it lowers onto the handle,
  stays, and she walks away (or sits down outside). *Privacy; autonomy; respecting a boundary.*
- **Waiting up** — dark hall, the front door opening slowly → the hall light comes on: MOM sitting on the stairs →
  SON frozen mid-step. *Trust; curfew; worry vs anger; repair after.*
- **Overheard** — MOM on the phone in the kitchen, talking about SON → SON stopped on the stairs, listening → his face
  falls. *What children overhear; labels; self-image.*
- **The laundry floor** — MOM walks in with a basket → the floor covered in clothes → SON looks up from his headphones
  with a slow blink. *Chores; picking battles.* (The clothes are objects on the floor, not worn.)

### Meals (one table outline)
- **Everyone on screens** — three faces lit by phones → the youngest pushes food round the plate, looking from one to
  the next → nobody looks up. *Phones and connection; modelling.*
- **"How was school?" "Fine."** — SON shrugs → MOM asks something specific instead (about the friend, the match) → his
  eyes come up from the plate. *Open questions; small bids; rituals of connection.*
- **The early exit** — SON picks up his plate and leaves mid-meal → MOM half-rises from her chair → and sits back down.
  *Withdrawal; conflict avoidance; choosing the moment.*
- **The parent's phone under the table** — MOM checks her phone below the table edge → SON sees it → his mouth pulls
  flat. *Double standards; modelling.*

### The car (a steering wheel outline, a seat line, a window line)
- **Side by side** — MOM driving, SON in the passenger seat → he says something real while looking out of the window →
  MOM's mittens tighten on the wheel, but she keeps her eyes on the road. *Talking side by side lowers the pressure;
  timing; listening.*
- **The mirror** (young child) — the child in the back seat → the parent's eyes in the rear-view mirror meet the child's
  (XCLOSE on the mirror strip) → the child smiles. *Being seen.*
- **Headphones on** — SON gets in and puts his headphones on at once → MOM's sentence stops mid-air → her mouth closes.
  *Withdrawal; timing.*
- **The late pick-up** — the parked car at night (NIGHT) → SON gets in, says nothing → MOM doesn't ask, starts the
  engine; SON's shoulders drop. *Trust; a safe way home; not interrogating.*

### The school gate (a railing and gate outline)
- **The hug that's too much** — MOM goes in for a hug → SON (preteen) steps back, glancing at his friends → MOM's open
  arms turn into a small wave. *Growing independence; peer status; not taking it personally.*
- **The hand that won't let go** (young child) — the child's mittens grip MOM's hand → MOM kneels to his level → his
  grip loosens a little. *Separation; a secure base.*
- **Reading the face** — SON walks out of the gate → MOM reads his face from across the road before he says a word →
  her smile softens into concern. *Attunement; noticing.*
- **Alone at the gate** — from the car: SON standing apart from a group by the railing → MOM's hand stops on the door
  handle. *Belonging; loneliness; when to step in.*

### Homework (a desk lamp outline, one sheet of paper)
- **The worn-out eraser** — SON at the desk → he rubs out the same line again and again, the paper thin → MOM in the
  doorway softens. *Perfectionism; fear of failure.*
- **The quick tab switch** — MOM passes behind → SON's laptop screen flicks to a grey worksheet → MOM's eyebrow lifts.
  *Honesty; trust; monitoring.*
- **Late-night collapse** — SON's head down on the desk at night → MOM sets a glass of water beside him and sits →
  he lifts his head. *Support vs rescue; stress.*
- **The good grade nobody saw** — SON puts a good test on the table → MOM says "great" without looking up from her
  phone → SON picks the test up again. *Attention; praise; being seen.*

### Sport (a ground line, a goal outline far off, a bench outline)
- **The look to the sideline** — SON misses the shot → his first glance is to MOM in the crowd, not the ball → MOM's
  face, the answer. *Performance pressure; conditional approval; fear of disappointing.*
- **The car ride after the loss** — MOM starts going through what went wrong → SON stares out of the window → MOM's
  mouth stops; she turns the radio down. *When to talk; not lecturing.*
- **The loud parent** — DAD shouting from the sideline, arms out → SON shrinks on the pitch → a team-mate glances at
  SON. *Pressure; embarrassment.*
- **The kit bag by the door** — the kit bag sits by the door, untouched, over several stills → MOM looks at it, then at
  SON. *Motivation; quitting; autonomy.*

### Phone and online
- **The buzz** — mid-conversation SON's phone buzzes → his eyes drop to it → MOM stops talking. *Attention; phones.*
- **The drawing held up** (young child) — the child holds up a drawing → MOM's eyes stay on her screen → the drawing
  lowers. *Attention; bids for connection; small moments add up.*
- **The glow at one in the morning** — a phone glow under the duvet (NIGHT) → MOM's silhouette in the doorway → the glow
  goes out a beat too late. *Sleep; social pressure; rules.*
- **Something upsetting** — SON's face lit by the screen, frozen → he puts the phone face-down → across the room MOM
  notices. *Online harm; safety to tell.*
- **The unlocked phone** — SON's phone left on the counter, unlocked → MOM's mitten hovering over it → she pulls it
  back, or doesn't. *Trust; privacy; monitoring.*
- **Left on read** — SON staring at one message bar with no reply → he refreshes again → he puts the phone face-down
  and lies back. *Social anxiety; belonging.*

### Bedtime (one bed outline, the door, the light switch)
- **One more question** (young child) — MOM's mitten on the light switch → "Mum?" from the bed → her hand freezes on the
  switch, she turns back. *Bedtime as the moment children open up.*
- **The talk in the dark** — lights off, MOM sitting on the edge of SON's bed → SON says the real thing to the ceiling →
  MOM doesn't move, doesn't turn the light on. *Timing; safety; less eye contact can help.*
- **The light under the door** — the landing at midnight → a strip of light under SON's door → MOM's hand stops on her
  own door handle. *Sleep; worry; trust.*
- **The nightmare** (young child) — the child in the doorway in the dark → MOM lifts the corner of her duvet → the child
  climbs in. *Comfort; safety.*

### Siblings
- **The comparison at the table** — MOM praises the sister's grade → SON's fork stops halfway → he looks at his plate.
  *Comparison; fairness.*
- **"He started it"** — two children each aiming a whole mitten at the other → MOM between them, eyes shut for a breath
  → she sits them down side by side. *Fairness; not taking sides.*
- **The big sister steps in** — the younger brother being teased at the gate → the older sister walks over and stands
  beside him → the teasing stops. *Belonging; roles.*
- **The new baby** — the older child watches MOM rocking the baby → the older child's drawing, held behind his back →
  MOM looks up and holds out her free arm. *Jealousy; sharing attention.*

### Grandparents
- **Different rules** — GRANDMA slides a biscuit across before dinner → MOM's one raised eyebrow → the child's grin.
  *Boundaries; consistency between generations.*
- **The video-call lesson** — SON teaches GRANDPA to video-call → GRANDPA's face far too close to the screen → SON's
  patient smile. *Roles reversed; patience; connection.*
- **The same words** — MOM visiting her own mother, who criticises her exactly the way MOM criticises SON → MOM's face as
  she hears it. *Patterns passed down.*
- **The empty armchair** — the family dinner with one armchair outline empty → the youngest keeps looking at it → MOM
  sees him looking. *Grief; how children carry loss.*

### Friends
- **"Everyone else is allowed"** — SON holding up his phone, "Jake's mum lets him" → MOM's arms crossed → SON's palms
  up. *Peer comparison; boundaries.*
- **Two selves** — SON laughing loudly on a video call → MOM walks in → the laugh drops off his face. *Teens need peers;
  the self they show at home.*
- **The party drop-off** — MOM: "Text me" → SON is already out of the car → MOM's mouth still open. *Trust;
  independence.* (Humour.)
- **The photo without him** — SON scrolling → he stops on a group photo he isn't in → his thumb hovers. *Exclusion;
  belonging.*
- **The friend at home** — SON's friend is polite and chatty with MOM → MOM tells a joke → SON's mittens over his face.
  *Embarrassment; identity.* (Humour.)

## 4. Method: turn any script line into a recognisable real moment

1. **Read the line for its idea, not its noun.** What claim, behaviour or feeling does it name? (`idea-not-object.md`)
2. **Find where a parent sees it.** Which age, which room, which time of day? Pick the most common and most specific
   instance ("the car ride home after the match", not "a conversation").
3. **Find the beat.** The one instant everyone recognises — usually a small action with a give-away detail.
4. **Find the reaction that carries the line's meaning.** Who sees it, and do they read it right or misread it?
5. **Decide how the picture relates to the line.** It can match it, extend it (show what the words leave out) or
   contradict it (irony or humour — the voice-over stays fixed).
6. **Choose the length.** One frame (beat and reaction together) for a short line; a short sequence of stills in one
   scene when the moment matters.
7. **Strip the place.** Keep only the outline cue that says where we are, and add another only when the action uses it (`style-and-colour-v19.md` §3.2); choose the colour element (the object that matters, or none); plan the
   camera (wider to set, closer for the reaction).
8. **Check recognition and maturity.** Would a parent say "that's my house"? Is it free of symbols? Is the child's pain
   never the joke?
9. **Check variety.** This situation, place and composition have not been used already in this video. Record the
   runner-up ideas in `alt` so later lines can use them.
10. **Record it** in the plan: `idea` (the situation in one line), `ln`, `ft`, `ia`, `dist`. Add new situations to §3
    after the video.

### Lines that sound abstract
Most "explanation" lines still have a real moment behind them. By line type (`ln`; the full list and how each type is
staged are in `camera-and-closeups-v19.md` §10):
- **statement** ("Teenagers' brains are still developing.") → the behaviour the claim explains: the teen who knows the
  rule and breaks it anyway, then stares at the dent in the car.
- **advice** ("Listen without fixing.") → the moment of doing it: MOM's mouth opens to advise, closes; she sits down.
- **model-sentence** ("You can tell me anything.") → the sentence said in a real place, and the reaction it gets.
- **adult-mirror** ("You would hate it if your boss did that.") → the same situation with the parent in the child's
  position, at work.
- **child-voice** ("I need you to listen.") → the child's face, and what he is looking at.
- **rapid-list** ("Grades. Friends. Sleep. Phone.") → one quick, real still per item, all with the same person.
- **time-jump** ("Years later…") → the same place or object, changed (the door, the height marks, the empty chair).
- **statistic or number** → never the number on screen; one person who is the number (one teen awake at one in the
  morning).

## 6. What to avoid
- A generic mood pose (sad on a sofa) instead of a specific moment.
- A symbol standing in for the moment (smile mask, empty speech bubble, story bubble, heart).
- Staging every situation at the same kitchen table. The last video held a large share of its frames in one kitchen
  layout. Use the place the moment really happens: the car, the gate, the stairs, the landing, the sideline, the
  doorway.
- The child's pain as the joke. Humour sits on the shared, familiar situation (`humour-text-contrast-v19.md`).
- Garment words, and words on screens.
- Two beats crammed into one frame. One recognisable beat; the reaction tells the viewer what it means.
